# Lessons from Madhopur Episode 1

Made on 25 November 2026 with Claude Code driving the Higgsfield short-drama workflow (Seedance 2.5 clips, 480p drafts, automatic edit).

## Planning

- **Approve a plan before spending.** A one-page scene, shot, reference and cost plan caught scope questions (one episode only, where it ends) before any credit was used.
- **Feed the workflow the screenplay, not the whole brief.** A production brief with tables, post notes and test prompts confuses the script checker. It read "five-year-old" in the notes as an adult character's age. Saving only the scene pages fixed it.
- **State ages in the workflow's own form.** Adults need an age in the user's words ("Suman 32") or a one-person sentence ("Meera's mother is an adult"). "Meera's mother" alone is not accepted as proof of an adult.
- **Children are pictured in neutral words.** The checker refuses the word "child" in a prompt for someone whose age the script does not state beside their name.
- **Seven clips covered 95 seconds.** Same-place clips were chained in pairs (a head clip and one continuation), which kept faces and light steady across the join.

## Prompts and generation

- **One block of prompt per clip, shots timed to the tenth of a second.** Each shot names who is in frame, where, and what moves. Shots with nobody in them need no subject.
- **Hindi lines need room.** Devanagari words are counted at 3.5 a second, so a nine-word line needs at least 2.6 seconds.
- **Restraint wording gets blocked.** "Tied to a post with rope around his chest" was refused by the safety filter and refunded. "His back against the post, his arms held behind it" passed and reads the same on screen.
- **Short action shots keep a crash readable.** Streak, eyes, brake pedal, wheels sliding, cab jolt, each one to two seconds.
- **A scripted beat can come back as a sliver.** "Devendra climbs out" played for 0.2 seconds at the end of a clip and had to be cut. Put important beats earlier in a clip and give them their own shot of 1.5 seconds or more.
- **Drafts first.** 480p drafts cost about 3 credits a second; 1080p finals quoted at about 16 a second on top.

## Editing

- **The first-three-seconds rule is an edit decision.** The fireball landed at 3.5 s in the raw clip; trimming the establishing shot put it at 2.9 s.
- **Trim still heads, keep reactions.** Only motionless establishing shots and one stray shot were removed, 8 seconds in all.
- **Do not cut through a loud sound.** A hard cut through the roar at the end of the maize-field clip was flagged; ending the shot half a second earlier on a near-white frame fixed it.
- **A white flash makes a clean scene change** out of a shot that already burns to white.
- **Speech recognition can hang on a wordless, noisy clip.** Marking wordless clips as having no speech, from the plan, got the edit moving again. Say so in the report.
- **Size on-screen text for a phone.** In a 1080-wide frame, 100 px names and 76 px captions read; 78 px and 46 px did not.
- **Subtitles come from the script.** The recogniser heard "दरो" for "डरो"; the script's spelling was used.

## What could not be checked

- Frames can be inspected one a second, but sound and motion cannot. A person still has to listen for specific cues (here, the metallic tick and the silence after the impact) and watch for jumpy cuts.
